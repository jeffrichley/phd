Dr. Kaipa directs the **Collaborative Robotics and Adaptive Machines Laboratory
(CRAMLab)** at ODU, which works toward fundamental advances in robotics across
autonomous systems, advanced manufacturing, and healthcare.

His research spans **swarm intelligence, human-robot collaboration, bio-inspired
robotics, surgical robotics, and robotics in education**. With his own PhD advisor he
co-developed **glowworm swarm optimization (GSO)**, on which he has published a book,
and his record includes more than eighty papers across journals, book chapters, and
refereed conference proceedings.

He serves as an **Associate Editor of IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)** and
of the ASME Journal of Computing and Information Science in Engineering. He holds a BE (Hons.) in Electrical Engineering from BITS Pilani and MS
and PhD degrees from the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, and is a member of
ASME, IEEE, and ASEE.

## Why this advisor for this dissertation

The dissertation's spine — bio-inspired undulatory locomotion driven by a mechanical
oscillator, with morphological computation doing part of the control — sits squarely in
Dr. Kaipa's bio-inspired robotics line. He teaches bio-inspired locomotion, and the
program's framing of embodiment-as-mechanism is one he can defend, not merely permit.
