## What it establishes

This is the review that makes the method question answerable in the committee's own literature
rather than in an adjacent one. It is published in *Annual Review of Fluid Mechanics*, it is
written for fluid dynamicists, and it treats machine learning as a technique available to the
field rather than as an import requiring justification. Its stated scope covers understanding,
modeling, optimizing and controlling flows, and it addresses "the strengths and limitations of
these methods from the perspective of scientific inquiry that considers data as an inherent part
of modeling, experimentation, and simulation."

For flow control specifically it goes further than tolerance. It records that reinforcement
learning "has been shown to outperform even optimal flow control strategies," citing Novati et al.
(2019), and identifies classes of flow control and optimization problems where learning
algorithms may be the appropriate instrument. It also traces the lineage back further than the
recent wave, noting neural network application to turbulence flow control pioneered by Lee et al.
(1997).

## What to pull

- **The legitimacy of the method is established inside the committee's own field**, not argued
  across a boundary into it. This is the citation that makes a lineage claim a statement of
  record rather than a request for permission (RQ1, RQ2).
- **The interpolation caution is the useful half of the limitation.** The review states that
  neural networks are fundamentally interpolative and are only well approximated within the span
  of their training data, so extrapolation warrants caution. That is a fluids-side statement of
  precisely the risk the dial-versus-switch distinction is about: warm-starting across a
  categorical regime boundary is an extrapolation, not an interpolation. The review names the
  hazard and does not measure it for an embodied swimmer.
- **"Physics informed but also physics consistent" is named as a major outstanding challenge**,
  which is the same standard the validation protocol applies to a simulator: specifying a physical
  term is not realizing it.

## Where it stops, in this dissertation's terms

The review's object throughout is the flow and its models. Where a body appears it is a boundary
condition or an actuator, not a learner accumulating a library. Nothing in it asks whether a
controller learned under one fluid coupling transfers to another, which is the question studies 2
and 4 exist to answer.
