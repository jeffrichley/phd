## What it establishes

HippoRAG 2 is the naming anchor for the dissertation's mechanism. It frames retrieval over
an external store as "non-parametric continual learning": retrieval-augmented generation
"retrieves relevant external information at inference time, allowing for real-time
adaptation to new knowledge without modifying the LLM itself," and "has quickly become the
de facto continual learning solution for production LLM systems." The system runs
Personalized PageRank over a knowledge graph built by the language model, adds deeper
passage integration and an online filter for irrelevant retrieved facts, and gains an
average of 7 percent on associative (multi-hop) tasks over standard retrieval. It
explicitly contrasts the three routes to continual learning: continual fine-tuning
(forgets, costly), model editing (localised, brittle), and retrieval (non-parametric,
scalable).

## What to pull

- The framing citation that legitimises "growing external memory = non-parametric
  continual learning" — the peer-reviewed version of the idea the dissertation transfers
  to motor skills (RQ2).
- The conceptual bridge: multi-hop associativity — chaining retrieved facts — is the text
  analog of skill composition, chaining retrieved motor skills; useful for presenting
  composition as multi-hop retrieval.
- Its three-way contrast of continual-learning routes, as the vocabulary for positioning
  the dissertation's comparison.

## Where it stops

Everything HippoRAG 2 stores and retrieves is text — knowledge-graph passages for factual,
multi-hop, and discourse question answering. There is no physical control, no robot, no
locomotion, and no fluid coupling; retrieval quality is measured on question answering,
never on whether a retrieved item helps acquire a dynamical controller. It names the
mechanism but does not run the controlled comparison against weight-update continual
learning that an embodied setting demands. The dissertation carries exactly this mechanism
from text to motor skills on a snake-form underwater robot and adds that comparison.
