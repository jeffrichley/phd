## What it establishes

MarineGym is a GPU-accelerated reinforcement-learning platform for underwater robotics
built on Isaac Sim with the PhysX physics engine. It contributes a custom hydrodynamics
plugin that computes the full Fossen wrench (added mass, added-mass Coriolis, damping, and
restoring forces) and applies it as an external force at the centroid of each submerged
link on every iteration. Throughput is approximately 250,000 frames per second on a single
RTX 3060, with more than 8,000 parallel instances. The shipped vehicles are rigid-body
underwater vehicles (BlueROV, BlueROV Heavy, LAUV, iAUV, HAUV). Its stated motivation is
that Isaac Sim "lacks native support for hydrodynamic forces and moments."

## What to pull

- The existence of a GPU per-link Fossen plugin for an Isaac simulator: this rules out any
  "first GPU hydrodynamics for Isaac" framing and fixes what the dissertation's platform
  chapter may claim (bears on RQ1's positioning).
- The throughput figure (~250,000 FPS, >8,000 instances) as the field's current bar for
  RL-viable marine simulation.
- Its validation posture: the only validation-adjacent statement is that the modelling
  approach "has been used and validated by other widely adopted underwater simulators":
  adoption cited in place of measurement. This is the gap RQ1's validation protocol fills.
- No articulated or snake-form vehicle appears; the lateral added-mass term that dominates
  an undulating body is never exercised.

## Where it stops

MarineGym shares the goal of fast marine dynamics on GPU but contains no validation
section: there is no comparison to potential-flow added mass, no closed-form Munk-moment
check, and no analytic baseline. Unvalidated is not the same as incorrect: the honest
statement is that its correctness is unestablished. It also targets the PhysX backend
rather than the Newton path Isaac Lab is moving to, and simulates only rigid-body vehicles,
so the fluid coupling that matters most to an undulating swimmer is never tested. The
dissertation's contribution is the validation and the Newton-path integration, not the idea
of GPU Fossen hydrodynamics.
