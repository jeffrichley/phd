## What it establishes

The title is the claim and the paper argues it in the venue that matters. It is four pages in
*IEEE Control Systems Magazine*, written for control theorists, by three of the people who built
reinforcement learning.

Its framing is a control taxonomy rather than a machine learning one. Control problems divide into
regulation and tracking, where the objective is to follow a reference, and optimal control, where
the objective is to extremize a functional of the system's behaviour. The authors observe that
adaptive methods for the first kind are well known while "optimal-control problems have received
relatively little attention," and that the adaptive optimal-control methods that do exist "are
almost all *indirect* methods, in which controls are recomputed from an estimated system model at
each step."

Their stated position: "We view reinforcement learning methods as a computationally simple, direct
approach to the adaptive optimal control of nonlinear systems."

The indirect and direct distinction is defined precisely. An indirect method "relies on a system
identification procedure to form an explicit model of the controlled system and determines then
the control rule from the model. Direct methods determine the control rule without forming such a
system model."

## What to pull

**The load-bearing sentence is in the Integrating Insights section**, and it is the justification
this dissertation needs rather than the definition:

> "When procedures for designing controls from a system model are computationally simple, as they
> are in linear regulation and tracking tasks, the distinction between indirect and direct adaptive
> methods has minor impact on the feasibility of an adaptive control method. However, when the
> design procedure is very costly, as it is in nonlinear optimal control, the distinction between
> indirect and direct methods becomes much more important."

That converts the method choice from a preference into a consequence. The direct approach earns
its place exactly where the model-based route is expensive, and the reason it is expensive here is
hydrodynamic: added-mass coupling that changes character across gait regimes is not available in
closed form. **The argument for the method is a fluid dynamics fact, and this paper is where the
control literature already concedes the premise.**

Also usable:

- The paper is explicit that these methods have roots in animal learning and connections "to
  theoretically justified methods for direct adaptive optimal control," so the lineage runs both
  ways and is acknowledged from the control side.
- Q-learning is described as "a direct adaptive method" that "does not estimate a system model" and
  "constructs the control rule directly," differing from Wheeler and Narendra's direct method in
  that respect.

## Where it stops, in this dissertation's terms

The authors are careful and their caveats are the useful part.

The convergence result they cite is Watkins's, and they state its condition: it holds "if all
actions continue to be tried from all states," which they call "a weak condition in the sense that
it would have to be met by any algorithm capable of solving this problem." That is a statement
about tabulated finite Markov decision problems.

On leaving that setting they are unambiguous: "it is not currently known whether theoretical
guarantees of convergence extend to various function representations, even representations in
which the estimated function values are linear in the representation's parameters. This is an
important area of current research."

And on scale: Q-learning "requires memory and overall computation proportional to the number of
state-action pairs. In large problems, or in problems with continuous state and action spaces which
must be quantized, these methods becomes extremely complex (Bellman's 'curse of dimensionality')."

So the paper licenses the *approach* and guarantees nothing about the *instance*. Cited for what it
establishes, which is that this is adaptive optimal control rather than an import, it is exactly
right. Cited as evidence that a learned controller will converge or transfer under fluid coupling,
it would be a misuse, and this dissertation does not need it to do that job.
