## What it establishes

Voyager is the demonstration the skill-memory thread rests on: a lifelong learning agent
whose knowledge lives in "an ever-growing skill library of executable code for storing
and retrieving complex behaviors," not in weights. Each verified behavior is committed as
a JavaScript program, keyed in a vector database by the embedding of its description;
facing a new task, the agent retrieves the top five relevant skills and composes them —
"complex skills can be synthesized by composing simpler programs." An automatic
curriculum (GPT-4 proposing the next task from the agent's state, an in-context form of
novelty search) and an iterative prompting loop (environment feedback, interpreter
errors, and a second GPT-4 verifying success before a skill is committed) close the loop.
The library is what sustains progress: Voyager discovers 3.3 times more unique items than
the best baseline, is the only method to reach diamond tools, and without the library the
ablation "exhibits a tendency to plateau in the later stages." The library also transfers
— dropped into a freshly generated world it solves unseen tasks from scratch, and grafted
onto AutoGPT it improves that system too, "a plug-and-play asset."

## What to pull

- The core forward-transfer evidence for RQ2 in its home domain: a growing, inspectable,
  compositional skill store demonstrably compounds capability and carries to new worlds
  when skills are programs.
- Design levers worth inheriting: retrieval by embedded skill description, composition of
  simpler skills into complex ones, commit-only-after-verification, and the
  library-versus-no-library ablation as the template for the dissertation's own.
- The verification gap as an RQ3 foil: Voyager's success critic is a language model
  reading inventory state, and the paper reports it misjudging outcomes — exactly the
  false-confidence failure a certified competence envelope is meant to close.

## Where it stops

Every Voyager skill bottoms out in calls to Mineflayer's high-level API — discrete
crafting, navigation, and combat primitives. The authors draw the line themselves: their
focus is "pushing the limits of GPT-4 for lifelong embodied agent learning, rather than
solving the 3D perception or sensorimotor control problems." So no skill is a dynamical
controller: executing one is deterministic API dispatch, not closed-loop control under
physics, and nothing in the system touches continuous dynamics, let alone fluid coupling.
The anti-forgetting claim is structural rather than empirical — stored code cannot decay
because nothing is ever trained, and no weight-update continual-learning baseline is run.
Competence is never certified: a committed skill carries no statement of where it works,
success is an LLM critic's judgment, and the limitations section concedes both the critic
and the curriculum err. The dissertation's question begins where this stops: whether the
library's forward transfer survives when the stored skills are controllers whose success
depends on the fluid they move through.
