## What it establishes

Where the 1992 paper asserts the identity, this one is the controls community absorbing it and
giving it vocabulary. It is an invited feature in *IEEE Circuits and Systems Magazine*, and its
stated purpose is "to show the usefulness of reinforcement learning techniques, specifically a
family of techniques known as Approximate or Adaptive Dynamic Programming (ADP) (also known as
Neurodynamic Programming), for the feedback control of human engineered systems." The authors are
explicit that this "requires bringing together ideas from two communities."

**The naming chain, which is the reason to cite this entry rather than only the 1992 one.** The
authors attribute four basic ADP methods to Werbos: reinforcement learning based on learning the
scalar value function is Heuristic Dynamic Programming (HDP); Action Dependent HDP, "introduced as
Q learning for discrete-state MDP by Watkins [1989]," learns the Q function and "allows one to
perform reinforcement learning without any knowledge of" the system dynamics. So Q-learning has a
controls-side name and a controls-side genealogy, and both are stated by control engineers in a
control venue.

**The motivating gap is stated in the same terms this dissertation uses.** The authors note that
classical design methods "are by nature off-line planning methods," give Riccati-equation design
for the LQR problem as the example, and state that "DP methods generally require the full knowledge
of the system dynamical equations." Against that, the Bellman formulation "leads to several
iterative methods for learning the solution of the optimal control equation without solving the
HJB equation, including Policy Iteration and Value Iteration," formulated "as on-line real-time
reinforcement learning methods for solving the optimal control problem using data measured" along
the trajectory.

## What to pull

- **The method has a controls name, a controls lineage and a controls venue.** Cited together with
  the 1992 paper, the pair establishes that this is not an import: one asserts the identity to
  control theorists, the other shows the field adopting and naming it seventeen years later
  (RQ2, RQ3).
- **"Without solving the HJB equation" is the mechanism, stated by control engineers.** The
  dissertation's justification for method is that the exact solution needs dynamics that
  added-mass coupling does not make available in closed form. This is the literature already
  saying that is the case where these methods are the instrument.
- **"DP methods generally require the full knowledge of the system dynamical equations"** is the
  premise half of that argument, in the field's own words.

## Where it stops, in this dissertation's terms

The developed results are strongest in the linear-quadratic setting: the actor network analysis
that the authors call "highly intriguing" is the LQR case, where the learned gain does not contain
the internal dynamics matrix. The nonlinear treatment is a survey of policy-iteration approaches
with citations rather than a guarantee for arbitrary nonlinear plants, and the continuous-time
nonlinear policy-iteration line is traced to Leake and Liu (1967) and Beard, Saridis and Wen
(1997) as feasible adaptive solutions rather than as settled ones.

More importantly for this dissertation: **the entire framing is one plant.** The question is
learning an optimal controller for a system whose dynamics are unknown. Nothing in it asks what
happens when the plant changes, whether a controller learned on one body informs one learned on
another, or whether some plant changes are categorical rather than parametric. That is the whole
of studies 2 and 4, and this literature does not reach it.
