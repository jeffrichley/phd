## What it establishes

Continual World builds a continual reinforcement learning benchmark from Meta-World: ten
simulated Sawyer-arm manipulation tasks trained in sequence for one million steps each,
then the same ten repeated (CW20), with soft actor-critic as the base learner. Its lasting
contribution is metrological: forward transfer is defined as the normalized area between a
method's learning curve on a task and a from-scratch reference curve, forgetting as the
end-of-sequence performance drop, and a reference transfer level (0.46 for CW20) sets the
bar that fine-tuning from the single best previous task would achieve. Evaluating seven
continual-learning methods, it finds that regularization approaches (elastic weight
consolidation, memory-aware synapses, variational continual learning) suppress forgetting
at the cost of negative forward transfer; replay methods collapse outright in the RL
setting; and parameter isolation (PackNet) performs best at 0.80 average success with zero
forgetting. No method approaches the reference transfer bar, and forward transfer on the
repeated second half drops even though the tasks are identical, which the authors suggest
may reflect loss of plasticity.

## What to pull

- The evaluation protocol: forward-transfer and forgetting definitions, the from-scratch
  reference curves, and the reference-transfer bar: the dissertation's skill-acquisition
  experiments can adopt these directly and stay comparable to the continual-RL literature.
- The gap stated in the field's own words: a model "can do better" than pairwise
  fine-tuning "by composing knowledge from multiple previous tasks": the benchmark names
  composition as the headroom but provides no mechanism for it.
- The empirical pattern that existing continual-learning methods sacrifice transfer to
  alleviate forgetting, which motivates asking whether skills kept outside the weights
  avoid the trade entirely.
- The strongest in-weights comparator this benchmark identifies: parameter isolation in
  the PackNet style.

## Where it stops

Embodiment is held constant by construction: all twenty tasks use the same arm, the same
twelve-dimensional observation space, and the same medium, so task variation is which
object or goal (parametric within one body), and nothing makes any pair of tasks
categorically different the way fluid coupling makes gait regimes categorical for a
swimming body. The authors state that they measure only low-level transfer through shared
network features and weights and do not explore compositionality of behavior or transfer
at a semantic level. Task boundaries are announced to the learner and each task gets its
own output head, so no method is ever asked to decide what situation it is in or what
prior skill applies: the retrieval question at the center of the dissertation is outside
the benchmark's frame.
