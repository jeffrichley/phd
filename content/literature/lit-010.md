## What it establishes

LOTUS builds an ever-growing skill library from a stream of manipulation tasks given a few
human demonstrations each. An open-vocabulary vision model extracts recurring temporal
segments from unsegmented demonstrations; incremental clustering decides whether each is a
new skill or an update to an existing one. Every skill is a goal-conditioned visuomotor
policy on raw images, and a meta-controller "invokes one skill by index, specifying its
behavior by generating subgoals at each time step." Training is hierarchical behaviour
cloning with experience replay. It improves success by 11 percent over the prior state of
the art and — importantly for the dissertation — measures both forward and backward
transfer. Its definition of a skill as a separately acquired, separately stored policy
module in a growing library is the literature-standard definition the dissertation adopts.

## What to pull

- The growing-library-plus-composition pattern as the closest robot-side analog for RQ2,
  and its forward/backward transfer evaluation as a reusable design reference.
- The composition-timing distinction: LOTUS composes stored skills at execution — the
  meta-controller picks a skill and subgoal each step — which maps to the dissertation's
  mission-time evaluation (bearing on RQ3), not to its acquisition-time headline. LOTUS
  never composes skills to learn a new one faster.
- Why its discovery mechanism does not transfer: skills come from segmenting human
  demonstration videos, inapplicable to a reinforcement-learning setting with no
  demonstrations — concrete support for prescribed skill boundaries instead.

## Where it stops

The store is opaque: a vision-model embedding plus policy weights, so selection can only be
nearest-neighbour, with no record of what a skill requires or produces. The library is
maintained by replay and gradient training, so it is a replay-based continual-learning
method rather than a frozen policy with an external retrieved memory, and it never
isolates retrieval against weight updates. The embodiment is fixed-base manipulation. The
dissertation's claim is that an inspectable store — cards carrying precondition and effect
metadata — is what enables principled selection and composition at acquisition time, the
thing LOTUS's opaque library cannot do.
