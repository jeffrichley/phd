## What it establishes

This is the bridge citation for study 3. Its central move is to put control theory and
reinforcement learning into one vocabulary, and its review covers three families: learning the
uncertain dynamics to safely improve performance, encouraging safety or robustness inside
reinforcement learning, and "methods that can formally certify the safety of a learned control
policy."

That third family is the one the operating-envelope work belongs to. The review's own framing is
that "machine learning techniques, particularly RL, can help generalization towards larger classes
of systems, while control theory provides the insights and frameworks necessary to provide
constraint satisfaction guarantees and closed-loop stability guarantees during the learning." An
envelope check that refuses a mission outside the library's coverage is a constraint-satisfaction
guarantee wrapped around a learned policy, which is the shape this literature already recognizes.

The review also grades safety rather than treating it as binary, and describes filtering
mechanisms such as model predictive safety certification, where a certifier sits outside a
learning-based controller and admits or corrects its output.

## What to pull

- **Certification of a learned policy is an established direction with its own vocabulary**, so
  study 3 can be stated in terms this literature already uses rather than introduced as novel
  machinery (RQ3).
- **The certifier-wrapping-a-learner architecture is the recognized pattern.** The envelope check
  is an instance of it, and the dissertation's contribution is what the certificate is *about*:
  coverage as set containment over a skill library, with the uncovered facet named.

## Where it stops, in this dissertation's terms

The review's first open challenge is scope of dynamics, and it names the exclusion directly: work
to date assumes a single nonlinear form, while robots also exhibit "hybrid dynamics," time-varying
dynamics, time delays, and "partial differential dependencies in the dynamics (e.g., in continuum
robotics)." Expanding safe learning control to those cases is stated as essential and unfinished.

**An undulating body under added-mass coupling is the partial-differential case.** So the review
establishes both that this is the right literature for study 3 and that study 3's setting is
outside what the literature currently covers, which is the shape a gap claim needs.

Two further limits are worth keeping. The authors note that many reviewed approaches "have only
been demonstrated on small toy problems and applying them to high-dimensional robotics problems is
not trivial." And they close by emphasizing "the need for realistic physics-based benchmarks to
facilitate fair comparisons," which is an argument for a validated simulator rather than against
one.
