## What it establishes

CLEAR trains an actor-critic on minibatches that mix new trajectories (for plasticity)
with trajectories replayed from a buffer (for stability), using an off-policy correction
for the distribution shift of old data plus two behavioral-cloning losses that pull the
network toward its own past self: a KL penalty between the historical and present policy
and an L2 penalty between the historical and present value estimates. The buffer is a
uniform reservoir sample of all past experience. On three DeepMind Lab tasks trained
cyclically in sequence, cumulative performance matches networks trained separately or
simultaneously (forgetting is effectively eliminated), and on a six-game Atari sequence
it beats elastic weight consolidation significantly and Progress and Compress slightly,
while being far simpler and needing no task labels. It also shows the buffer can shrink to
one stored experience in two hundred at only slight cost, and that some fresh on-policy
data is load-bearing: at one hundred percent replay, a newly introduced task learns
markedly slower the later it appears in the stream.

## What to pull

- The replay baseline paper 2's continual-learning family requires: the closest
  weight-update analog to an external memory, and the fairest one for task-free
  evaluation, since it never needed task identity in the first place.
- Its own concessions, useful for positioning: where storing a buffer is prohibitive,
  parameter-protection methods may be preferable; cloning toward an outdated policy can
  be actively harmful when the task's action semantics shift.
- The interference-versus-forgetting distinction (section 4.1), a clean framing for why
  sequential acquisition, not multi-task incompatibility, is the failure mode that
  matters in a lifelong stream.

## Where it stops

CLEAR's target is forgetting, not transfer: nothing carries a source skill toward a new
task beyond whatever the shared weights incidentally provide. Its buffer is not a memory
in any inspectable sense: raw transitions sampled uniformly, with no record of what was
learned, no selection by relevance, and no way to ask which past experience bears on the
present task. Task-agnosticism is its headline virtue, which means task variation is one
undifferentiated stream by design; the question of whether transfer benefit is flat or
scaling in task similarity cannot even be posed inside the method. Its evaluation is
vision-based game and navigation reinforcement learning: no locomotion, no continuous
control, and none of the fluid coupling that makes gait variation categorical for a
swimmer.
