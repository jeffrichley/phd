## What it establishes

This is the book behind the phrase "Fossen's vectorial model": the standard equation
structure for a rigid body moving through water, in which four hydrodynamic terms act on
the rigid-body dynamics: the added-mass matrix (an acceleration reaction), the
added-mass Coriolis term that produces the Munk moment, velocity-dependent damping, and
the restoring wrench of gravity and buoyancy. Derived in a vectorial setting from
Lagrangian and Newtonian mechanics for ships, high-speed craft, submarines, and
underwater vehicles, it is the common language of marine simulation: when a simulator or
plugin claims marine-grade hydrodynamics, this model is what it claims to provide. The
dissertation's fidelity question is posed in exactly these terms: which of Fossen's
four terms a GPU learning simulator actually realises, and which interface each term can
enter the solver through.

## What to pull

- The model decomposition itself, which structures RQ1's measurement: the
  velocity-dependent terms (damping, Munk moment, restoring) can be computed before the
  solve and handed to the solver as forces, while the added-mass reaction depends on the
  acceleration the solver has not yet computed, so fidelity splits along Fossen's own
  term boundaries.
- The citation for the model structure wherever the dissertation writes the marine
  dynamics equations; potential-flow coefficient values come from Lamb and Brennen, not
  from this book.
- The shared-specification framing: prior GPU marine simulation work implements the same
  Fossen model, which is what makes a fidelity comparison between implementations
  well-posed rather than apples-to-oranges.

## Where it stops

The handbook defines the model and the control theory built on it; realisation inside a
physics engine is outside its scope. It does not address articulated bodies undulating
through the fluid, GPU solvers, or the structural question of where an
acceleration-dependent reaction can enter a time-stepped simulation, the gap the
dissertation's first study occupies. Its evidence status in the program is a resolved
registry record (ISBN-verified), not a held PDF, so it is cited only for the model
structure, never for page-level claims or numeric coefficients.
