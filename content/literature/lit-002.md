## What it establishes

This is documentation used as primary evidence, not background reading: the dissertation's
first validation claim is a claim about this page. The page specifies MuJoCo's ellipsoid
fluid model as five components: added mass, viscous drag, Magnus lift, Kutta lift, and
viscous resistance. The added-mass component is defined from potential-flow theory
"Following Chapter 5 of Lamb," with coefficients taken from Tuckerman's 1925 ellipsoid
inertia factors: the same source and the same numbers as the analytic targets the
validation protocol uses (axial 1.115, lateral 1.345 effective-mass ratios for the platform
geometry). The specification includes an explicit acceleration-reaction term, and the page
notes that unlike drag and lift, "the added mass term cannot be scaled": it has no tuning
coefficient a user could have set to zero.

## What to pull

- The gap the validation study measures is against the model's own declared theory, not
  against a preferred external reference: the docs cite Lamb, and so does the protocol.
  This closes the reviewer escape "you validated it against the wrong reference" (RQ1).
- The claim wording this source enforces: the correct statement is
  specified-but-not-realised, never "MuJoCo omits added mass": the acceleration reaction
  is present in the specification, and a claim of omission would be trivially refuted by
  anyone opening the page.
- Added mass cannot be scaled or disabled via configuration, so its absence from effective
  mass is structural, not a settings error.
- The silence itself: no statement anywhere on the page says the term is approximate,
  lagged, or excluded from effective mass.

## Where it stops

The page presents added mass as a complete physical component and says nothing about
whether the specified acceleration reaction is realised in the simulated body's effective
inertia. That silence is the finding: the free-body measurement (effective mass 1.000
against Lamb's 1.115 axial and 1.345 lateral) shows specification and realisation diverge,
and the documentation gives a user no way to discover this. One caveat travels with the
citation: the capture is from the moving "stable" URL and must be re-pinned to the exact
MuJoCo version used before submission.
