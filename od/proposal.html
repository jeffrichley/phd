1.0

Abstract

proposal.abstract · required

150–250 words. The whole argument compressed: the situation, the specific failure, what you will build, how you will know it worked. An advisor who reads only this paragraph should be able to say yes or no.

On this section Write it last. An abstract drafted before §1.6 exists is a description of an intention, not of a contribution.
1.1

Problem statement

proposal.problem · required

The concrete situation a deployed embodied agent is in, stated without reference to any method. What task distribution shifts, over what horizon, and what breaks when it does. Name a real setting rather than "real-world tasks".

proposal.problem.stakes

Optional. Why the failure matters — cost, safety, or scientific consequence. One paragraph, no adjectives that a reviewer could ask you to defend.

Test If this section could open a paper by any of the four authors in §05, it is not yet your problem statement.
1.2

The gap

proposal.gap · required

Not "little work exists". Name the three or four closest lines of work, grant each its actual achievement, then identify the precise assumption each one makes that does not hold in §1.1. Every claim here should point at an entry in §05.

Dependency This section cannot be written before §05 has entries. The gap is a claim about the literature and needs the literature underneath it.
1.3

Thesis statement

proposal.thesis · required · one sentence

A single declarative sentence that could be wrong. Of the form: “<mechanism> enables <capability> under <conditions>, measured by <criterion>.” This sentence is the dissertation. Everything below is apparatus for testing it, and it is the line the committee will quote back to you.

Falsifiability Ask what result would make this sentence false. If nothing would, it is a research area, not a thesis.

Approach

proposal.approach · required

The method sketch. Enough formalism that a reader can tell what is new, not so much that it becomes a paper. Say what is borrowed and from where; say what you are inventing and why the borrowed thing was insufficient.

Scope guard Anything described here that is not tested in §1.7 belongs in §1.10 as future work, not here.

1.7

Evaluation plan

proposal.evaluation · required

Environments, baselines, metrics, seed counts, statistical treatment. Commit in advance to what counts as success — pre-registering the bar is the cheapest defense against a reviewer who later moves it.

proposal.evaluation.baselines

Named baselines and why each is the fair comparison, not the convenient one. Runs appear in §03 as they complete.

Pre-registration Once approved, this section becomes the contract. Changing a metric after seeing results is legitimate — but it goes in §08 with a reason.

Resources & schedule

proposal.resources

Compute, hardware, robot access, data, collaborators, funding. Be specific about what is already secured versus what is assumed.

The dated schedule is maintained in §06 and summarised into this document at version cut.

Common objection “Is this feasible in the time available?” is the second question every committee asks. Answer it here, in months, not adjectives.
1.10

Scope boundaries

proposal.scope · required

What this dissertation is not. Explicitly excluded settings, assumptions you are granting yourself, and adjacent problems you are deliberately leaving alone. This section prevents scope creep during the defense.

Why it earns its space A crisp exclusion list converts an open-ended objection into an already-answered one.
